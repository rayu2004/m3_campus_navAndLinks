Link Best Practices

  • The text inside your link should tell the user what will happen when they click it.
  • Link text should never say something like “click here”.
    • Bad: Click here to submit our form.
    • Good: You can contact us by submitting a message through our form.
  • Test your links to make sure they work from every page.
  • Use the appropriate type of link when adding one to your page.

About Links

Today you will be learning about and creating six types of hyperlinks:

  • absolute
  • relative
  • fragment
  • email
  • telephone
  • relative with fragment
  • back to top

Absolute Hyperlinks

Absolute hyperlinks are used when links are needed to another website. For example, here is an absolute link to ASU: https://www.asu.edu/.

Relative Hyperlinks

Relative hyperlinks are used when links are needed to a page within your own website. Try: About and Contact.

E-Mail Hyperlinks

Email hyperlinks will open the user’s default mail program. Example: Email Professor Barnett and Email the Webmaster.

Telephone Number Hyperlinks

Clicking this from a smartphone will open the dialer: (555) 555-5555.

Fragment Hyperlinks

Fragment links jump to a specific part of the same page. Try this mini table of contents: Best Practices About Navigation About Links Absolute Relative Email Telephone

Combining Relative and Fragment Hyperlinks

You can link to a different page and jump to a section on that page. Example: go to About (top).
